Daymark · Privacy Policy · Effective November 3, 2026

Your plans stay on your iPhone.

Daymark is a personal secretary for the day in front of you. It holds schedules, tasks, notes, routines, and private writing. This policy explains what the current iPhone app stores, what stays on the phone, and what can leave the phone.

The short version

  • There is no Daymark account, no advertising, and no sale of your information.
  • The developer cannot sign in and read the plans on your phone.
  • Ordinary plans are saved on the phone. The Private notebook is encrypted with a passphrase you choose. Daymark cannot recover that passphrase.
  • Janice and speech reading use Apple’s on-device tools. The selected text stays on the phone.
  • If you tap Save link, Daymark sends only that public video link to the site’s public preview service for a title and thumbnail. Your workspace is not included.

Who is responsible

Daymark is published by Gregory Mark Tullis. Use the support page linked from the App Store listing if you need to reach the developer. Do not send a copy of your workspace unless you choose to.

Information you put into Daymark

Daymark stores only what you enter, import, or ask it to read from a file you choose. That can include an optional name, wake and sleep times, calendar events, tasks, routines, goals, shopping lists, vehicle records, notes, a Janice conversation you choose to keep, an imported transcript, and a public video link with its returned title and thumbnail.

The Private notebook is stored only as an encrypted envelope. Daymark does not ask for your contacts, photos library, precise location, or advertising identifier.

What is not collected by the developer

“Collected” here means information transmitted to the developer or to a server the developer operates. This version does not do that.

  • No Daymark account, server profile, analytics tool, crash-reporting service, or advertising network.
  • No sale, rental, or exchange of personal information.
  • No training of a cloud model on your notes.

Where information is stored

The working copy is saved in the app’s private storage on this iPhone. After a successful save, Daymark also writes a separate recovery file on this phone and keeps the previous recovery file. Those files are not uploaded. If the main database cannot be read, Daymark restores from the recovery file instead of starting over. A healthy newer copy is not replaced by an older one.

Deleting the app deletes these files. An export you save somewhere else, or an Apple device backup that includes the app, can outlive the app.

The Private notebook

The Private notebook is encrypted on the phone before it is saved. The passphrase is not sent to a server. There is no recovery service and no forgot-passphrase reset. If you forget the passphrase, that notebook cannot be opened. Janice, search, and planning are not given its contents.

Janice

If you ask Janice, the question and the planning section you choose are shown to Apple’s on-device model. That text stays on the phone. It is not sent to Daymark or to a cloud model, and it is not added to a developer log. Janice can be wrong. A suggestion does not change your calendar until you approve it.

Video and audio files you import

If you import a video or audio file, Daymark reads speech on this iPhone and can sample written words on the screen. The file is then deleted. The transcript can be saved in your workspace if you keep it. It is not uploaded.

A social link is not the video. Save link can request a public title and thumbnail from that site. It does not download the video or its audio. Daymark does not watch a demonstration and does not understand movement.

Accepted files include MP4, MOV, M4V, 3GP, MPEG, and common audio files, up to 512 MB and 10 minutes. WebM, MKV, and AVI are not readable on this iPhone.

Links you open

If you tap a web link inside Daymark, the iPhone may open it in Safari. That visit is between you and the site. Daymark does not attach your workspace to it.

Apple’s device backup

Daymark does not operate iCloud sync. If you have turned on Apple’s encrypted iPhone backup, Apple may include this app’s local files in that backup under Apple’s own terms. Daymark cannot read Apple’s backup.

Exports you create

An export is a file you choose to save. Ordinary notes in that file are readable by anyone who has the file. The Private notebook remains encrypted. Daymark does not upload the export.

Reminders

Reminders appear while Daymark is open. This version does not send an alert after you leave the app. Do not rely on Daymark as your only alarm.

Children

Daymark is not directed to children under 13, and it does not offer accounts. A parent may type a family schedule into their own phone. That information stays under the same on-phone rules.

How long information stays

Information stays on the phone until you delete the entry, replace the workspace, delete the app, or erase the phone. Recovery history keeps older saved versions so a mistake can be reviewed. Export anything you cannot afford to lose.

How to delete information

  • Delete a note, task, or event inside Daymark.
  • Clear a Janice conversation from its own control. Older recovery versions may still contain it until those versions age out or you delete the app.
  • Delete the app to remove the on-phone database, recovery files, and drafts.
  • Delete exports and old device backups yourself if you want those copies gone too.

Changes

If a later version sends more information off the phone, this page will say so before that version asks you to use it.
